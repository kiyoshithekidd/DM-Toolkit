# DM's Toolkit, Personas

Personas for prioritizing the build. The main persona is the DM running homebrew campaigns at the kitchen table for a small home party. Flavor is a requirement, not decoration: the app should feel like a bard's toolkit, not a spreadsheet.

## Main persona: The Kitchen Table DM

- Who: A DM running homebrew campaigns at the kitchen table for a small party, usually family. Sessions are short, squeezed between everything else, and the game has to keep moving or the players drift.
- What they need: monsters first. Fast lookup, HP and initiative tracking that takes seconds, and the combat rules a tap away when a question comes up. The dice roller has to feel like real dice. The reference has to answer the question before the moment passes.
- What they don't need: player tools, character sheets, multiplayer, or anything that turns the 3DS into a second game. The app is a screen for the DM, not a toy for the table.
- How the app serves them: the fight tracker is the home screen. Search is the fastest path in. Everything is built for one hand on the 3DS while the other holds notes or dice.

## Secondary persona: The Homebrew DM

- Who: A DM running custom campaigns who invents as much as they look up. They lean on generators: NPC names, loot, random encounters.
- What they need: generators that are fast and surprising, and a way to add their own homebrew monsters and entries later.
- What they don't need: a locked-down official-only reference. If it can't hold homebrew, it doesn't fit.
- How the app serves them: generators on the home screen, homebrew entries as a later slice.

## Secondary persona: The New DM

- Who: Someone running their first campaign. They know the rules roughly and get stuck when a spell or condition comes up mid-fight.
- What they need: the rules reference to be forgiving and fast, and the encounter generator to keep them from accidentally killing the party.
- What they don't need: depth they can't use yet. The manual should not bury them.
- How the app serves them: the pocket SRD with plain entries, and the generator tuned to party level.

## Tentative persona: The Young DM

- Who: A teen DM running games for friends, learning the role by doing it.
- What they need: the same fast reference and a simple encounter builder, plus a low price of failure. Nothing in the app should punish a wrong tap mid-game.
- Status: tentative. Not a design driver until confirmed.

## Anti-persona: The Screen-Free Purist

- Who: A DM who believes no screens belong at the table. Paper, books, and dice only.
- What this means for the app: do not try to convert them. The app wins by being fast and unobtrusive, so it never becomes the thing that slows the game down. If the app ever makes the DM stare at the screen instead of the players, it has failed its own main persona too.

## Prioritization rule

- The Kitchen Table DM decides. When a feature helps them at the table, it goes first. When it helps someone else but slows them down, it waits.
- Monsters first, then dice and generators, then the reference depth. That order comes from how they actually play.
